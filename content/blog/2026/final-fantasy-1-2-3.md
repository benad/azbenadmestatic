In 2024 I bought the newly released [Final Fantasy Pixel Remaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Fantasy_Pixel_Remaster) bundle for Xbox Series X/S. I contains Final Fantasy I to VI, originally released on Nintendo's Family Computer ("Famicom") and Super Famicom in Japan, and a few translated and released on the NES and SNES in North America.

I bought that collection because I don't think I've fully finished most of them before. I remember having finished [Final Fantasy I & II: Dawn of Souls](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Final_Fantasy_I_%26_II:_Dawn_of_Souls) on the GameBoy Advance. I played IV and VI on the SNES through rentals, so I never had the time to fully finish either from start to end in a single save file. I played most of the first half of VI on the iOS port, but as it was a 32-bit iOS app it was eventually de-listed from the store.

The Pixel Remaster series isn't reusing the enhanced graphics of those previous re-releases, nor does it have any of the "additional content" (in the form of optional dungeons and bosses near the end of the game) that previous re-releases added as an incentive to buy them. But then, the reworked graphics are better and still match closely the original designs, which, by the way, are bundled as extras. The remastered music is excellent, often recorded with live instruments, and while there is an option to revert to the original mixes I really enjoyed the modern versions. The games also have "quality of life" enhancements that make those older games more accessible to modern players, like auto saves and speeding up battles.

So, I started playing each Final Fantasy game in that collection, in order. I thought that only those "2D retro" entries of Final Fantasy were widely release outside of PlayStation consoles, apart maybe from a few that were released on PC, like the FF VII version I played a [long time ago in 2014](https://benad.me/blog/2014/12/29/final-fantasy-vii-a-late-review/). What I didn't notice since is that all the single-player "mainline" Final Fantasy games were eventually ported to Xbox One in one form or another between 2016 and 2019. Even XVI was only briefly exclusive to the PlayStation 5 and was then released on Xbox Series X/S last June.

Recently, there was a sale that heavily discounted all Final Fantasy games on Xbox, so I bought them all for cheaper than what it would cost me to pre-order Grand Theft Auto VI.

I've just finished Final Fantasy V, but for this post I'll focus on the 3 games that were released on the Famicom.
